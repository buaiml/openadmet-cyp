# CYP Direct-Inhibition Prediction: Method Report

## Task and outputs

The method predicts direct-inhibition potency for CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2D6 and CYP3A4 from molecular structure. Predictions are reported as pIC50, the negative base-10 logarithm of IC50 in molar units. The test output contains one row per supplied molecule, preserving the original SMILES, molecule name and order, with four floating-point potency predictions.

## Molecular representations and supervision

The ensemble combines complementary structure representations: counted Morgan fingerprints, physicochemical descriptors, learned graph representations, molecular-transformer representations and protein–ligand complex features. Models use direct-inhibition observations together with auxiliary screening, potency and solubility supervision. Auxiliary targets retain their source-specific units and masks rather than being treated as interchangeable direct-inhibition labels.

The added potency network uses 3,289 input coordinates: 2,048 chiral radius-two log-transformed Morgan counts, 217 ordered RDKit descriptors, 768 frozen Uni-Mol2 84M molecular embeddings from a single free conformer, and 256 zero coordinates. Descriptor preprocessing, embedding centering and scaling are taken from the corresponding training fold. The hydrogen-bond-acceptor descriptor uses the definition matching the saved network's training inputs.

## Frozen ensemble

The base predictor, `P0`, is an equal-weight combination of two complete predictors:

- A molecular-transfer ensemble comprising five-member Chemprop families, an eight-estimator TabICL predictor with a 0.60 quantile readout, and CYP2D6-specific Chemprop specialists. Member, seed and fold reductions are fixed.
- A heterogeneous ensemble of support-vector kernel models, tree and neural predictors. Its components include count/descriptor/property kernels, auxiliary-screening-profile readouts, solubility-transfer networks, KERMT and pADME graph networks, and Boltz-derived complex features. Component weights are enzyme-specific. Some terms are signed residual corrections or query-dependent gates rather than convex averages.

The property-kernel component includes a deterministic randomized-bandwidth variant: a hash of the transformed property vector selects a quantile of the eligible training-width distribution. This is a repeatable kernel parameterization, not an estimate of molecular uncertainty.

A separate task-specific neural predictor, `H`, has a shared 64-unit hidden layer and 32-unit enzyme-specific heads. It uses a physical-unit Huber point/interval objective with auxiliary outputs; only the four direct-inhibition outputs enter the final prediction. Inference uses the saved exponential-moving-average states at epoch 163, without further optimization.

The final predictor is `P(x) = (1 - a) P0(x) + a H(x) + delta(x)`, where `a = 0.019882123849455622`.

## Fixed teacher-member covariance correction

For each enzyme and fold, five saved point-prediction teachers supply individual forecasts `p_m(x)`. Four local training-neighborhood summaries `s_mj(x)` are computed using the same teacher member's supervised training pool. The correction retains the interaction lost when teacher forecasts and neighborhood features are averaged before a bilinear readout:

For each neighborhood feature `j`, compute `c_j(x) = mean_m[p_m(x) × s_mj(x)] - mean_m[p_m(x)] × mean_m[s_mj(x)]`, using the five matching teacher members.

Each fold contains 32 saved bootstrap readouts per enzyme. Their raw interaction coefficients map this covariance to a potency correction. Two absolute functions are evaluated: the member-covariance predictor and its matched mean-feature predictor. Each is averaged separately across the five folds in float64, in fold order; `delta` is their difference. That difference is added with coefficient exactly +1. The implementation preserves the saved feature transforms and addition/subtraction order, without coefficient collapsing, clipping or additional output rescaling.

## Training, validation and test inference

Model assessment uses five structure-grouped folds. Held-out identities and their protected aliases are excluded from the corresponding supervised training sources; the separately reserved pseudo-test identities remain excluded. Training-derived scalers, neighborhood pools and model states are fold-specific. Development and hyperparameter selection use the designated first fold; selected procedures are frozen before the complete five-fold evaluation.

On the internal five-fold benchmark, covering 18,868 molecule–enzyme queries and 6,202 assay measurements, the final predictor obtains **weighted macro ST-RAE 0.6094719427161902** and **unweighted macro ST-RAE 0.5715334964184702**. ST-RAE means soft-threshold relative absolute error. The **weighted** macro is an internal research selection metric; the **unweighted** macro follows the challenge metric convention. Both numbers are internal development results, not blinded challenge-test scores. Repeated validation-guided selection means they do not constitute independent evidence of generalization.

Test inference loads frozen states and training transforms, completes each own-fold function before its declared reduction, and performs no retraining or test-label-based selection. The delivered 750-row Parquet has string identifiers and four finite float64 pIC50 columns. Its SHA-256 is `f4eacac9798f75ff0ffa1057165e6232823af47526968fac8d0b45d5b39ad374`.
