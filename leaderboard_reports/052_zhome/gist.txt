# Innovation in ML Award — contribution in three minutes

**Claim:** We used public endpoint-level Live feedback as weak, assumption-dependent evidence of CYP regression distribution shift, then applied a restrained, rank-preserving recalibration that improved the observed Live MA-ST-RAE while openly reporting that our local shift proxies rejected it.

## Problem

The OpenADMET CYP challenge asks for direct-inhibition pIC50 predictions across four isoforms on a blinded, 750-compound set. Its primary metric, macro-averaged soft-threshold relative absolute error (MA-ST-RAE), measures distance outside assay-derived credible intervals and gives zero error inside them. A strong molecular ranking can still have poor absolute error if a new chemistry population shifts its activity level or spread. Our `reg_v30` model showed this tension: the published Live endpoint scores were **0.9114** for CYP1A2 and **1.1669** for CYP2D6, despite Spearman correlations of **0.7113** and **0.4325**, respectively. Hidden labels, scoring masks, and the complete official ST-RAE backend were unavailable. The practical question was whether published aggregate feedback could support a carefully bounded adjustment without claiming to know the hidden targets. [Official challenge description](https://openadmet.org/blog/announcing-openadmets-cyp-inhibition-blind-challenge/); [local baseline record](../reg_v32/REPORT.md).

## Method

We treated the Live **endpoint-level** MAE, R², and Spearman, together with the empirical distribution of our 750 blind-set predictions, as constraints on possible target distributions. Under an explicit linear conditional model with independent residuals, we solved separately for positive and negative target-mean offsets and examined three residual-tail assumptions. The solution preserves the empirical prediction shape; it does not assume a Gaussian prediction marginal. Several scenarios fit the same public aggregates. In particular, the sign of the target shift is **not identified** by those aggregates. The negative branch was an operational prior, not an observed property of the blind set. [Inference and sensitivity record](../reg_v32/REPORT.md); [reference solver](../release/shift_inference.py).

The action was deliberately smaller than the negative-branch conditional optimum: we halved both its proposed shift and its expansion beyond unit scale. For an endpoint prediction *p*, we applied `p′ = mean(p) + s[p − mean(p)] + δ`. CYP1A2 used `s = 1.391473, δ = −0.419698`; CYP2D6 used `s = 1.474552, δ = −0.662590`. CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 were unchanged. Positive scales preserve every within-endpoint rank, so this tests calibration rather than a new molecular ranking model. No hidden labels entered the transform, no new regressor was fitted, and no extremes were clipped. The main risk is straightforward: choosing the wrong direction, residual law, or interval behavior can make the same transform harmful. Repeated use of Live feedback can also overfit that evaluation population. [Frozen transform](../release/recalibrate.py); [candidate record](../reg_v32/REPORT.md).

## Evidence, including the counterexample

| Comparison (lower is better) | Positive Live result | Honest local negative result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `reg_v30` parent → `reg_v32` mild, MA-ST-RAE | **0.7677 → 0.5743** on Live feedback | Scaffold proxy **0.677159 → 0.953080**; fixed-shift proxy **0.718801 → 0.744157** |

The Live result is an observed comparison on the Live evaluation population, not a predicted score. The two local results use the documented proxy denominator and different test scenarios; **both got worse**, so they did not independently validate the action. The scaffold folds follow the training distribution. Our frozen stress scenario shifts CYP2D6 only **−0.5** and cannot reproduce the hidden joint distribution of targets, residuals, and assay intervals. This mismatch is an informative failure of our proxy design, not a reason to erase it. The Live endpoint scores after mild recalibration were **0.6495** for CYP1A2 and **0.6551** for CYP2D6; the two untouched endpoints remained **0.4803** and **0.5124**. [Local and Live audit](../reg_v32/REPORT.md); [record of the mild Live endpoint values](../reg_v36/REPORT.md).

## Why this fits the award

The [organizers' award criteria](https://openadmet.org/blog/announcing-openadmets-cyp-inhibition-blind-challenge/) explicitly value creative use of available data, novel training or uncertainty strategies, simple effective approaches, and ideas beyond leaderboard rank. Our contribution is a reproducible *evaluation-and-decision method*: convert public aggregate metrics into multiple conditional shift hypotheses, expose their non-identifiability, choose a guarded monotone action, and publish the local contradiction alongside the Live result. It uses information available to every entrant, with no proprietary labels. The lightweight affine layer is simple and auditable. Our scenario sweep is a way to **reason about** uncertainty from shift assumptions; it is **not** calibrated uncertainty quantification. The accompanying [code package](../release/README.md) includes the solver, frozen recalibrator, and a synthetic-data demonstration.

## What we are not claiming

We are not claiming an original neural architecture, discovery of the hidden target mean, a statistically identified direction of shift, a confidence interval, or superiority on the fully blinded final population. We are not claiming that the local proxies supported this recalibration; they did the opposite. We are not claiming the compact public code recreates the historical fitted ensemble without its original version chain and inputs. The Live gain is real as reported, but its scope is the Live population and the public feedback used to select this action.
