CYP Inhibition Blind Challenge — Method Report
Submission to the OpenADMET CYP Inhibition Blind Challenge.
Tracks entered: Direct Inhibition (regression) and Time-Dependent Inhibition (classification).
Structure-prediction track not entered.
Reproduce with `python cyp_pipeline.py` (single script, ~10 min on CPU).
---
1. Summary
A deliberately simple baseline: Morgan count fingerprints plus physicochemical
descriptors, fed to per-isoform LightGBM models. No pretraining, no graph neural
network, no proprietary data. The goal was a clean, honestly-validated pipeline
rather than a maximally tuned one, given a one-day build window.
The two decisions that mattered most were not architectural:
Scaffold-based cross-validation instead of random splits. The challenge
test set is analog expansion around training hits, so random CV is
optimistically biased. All numbers reported here are scaffold-split
out-of-fold.
Tuning the TDI decision threshold for MCC. This more than doubled CYP3A4
MCC relative to the default 0.5 cutoff — a larger gain than any model change
produced.

The training matrix is sparse; the test matrix is dense. Only 41 of 4,905
training compounds carry all four labels, yet predictions are required for all
four on every test compound. This mismatch is the defining structural feature of
the task and shapes the limitations discussed in §6.
---
3. Featurization
Per compound, 2,062 features:
Morgan count fingerprint, radius 2, 2,048 bits (RDKit
`rdFingerprintGenerator`, ECFP4-equivalent — matching the similarity metric
OpenADMET used to construct the test set by analog expansion).
14 physicochemical descriptors: MW, cLogP, TPSA, HBD, HBA, rotatable
bonds, aromatic rings, total rings, Fsp3, heavy atoms, heteroatoms, ring
count, molar refractivity, Bertz complexity.
All 4,905 training and 750 test SMILES parsed successfully; no compounds dropped.
---
4. Models
Track 1 — Direct inhibition (regression)
Four independent `LGBMRegressor` models, one per isoform, each fit only on rows
with a non-null label for that isoform.
Objective: `regression_l1`, chosen to match the absolute-error family of the
ST-RAE scoring function. Key parameters: 1,200 estimators, lr 0.03, 63 leaves,
`colsample_bytree` 0.5 (fingerprints are sparse and highly correlated),
`subsample` 0.8, `reg_lambda` 1.0. Early stopping on the held-out fold, patience
100. Final models refit on all available data for that isoform.
Track 2 — TDI (classification)
`LGBMClassifier` on `CYP3A4_is_TDI` and `CYP2D6_is_TDI` (the two scored
isoforms). Positive rate ≈21% for both. The decision threshold was selected by
grid search over [0.02, 0.98] maximizing MCC on scaffold-held-out predictions,
rather than defaulting to 0.5.
Validation
Bemis–Murcko scaffolds, greedily packed into 5 folds largest-first, so whole
scaffold series stay together. 4,530 distinct scaffolds across 4,905 training
compounds.

6. Findings
Performance tracks label count almost exactly. CYP3A4 has the most labels
(2,335) and the best R² (0.561); CYP2D6 has 1,493 and the worst (0.142). CYP2D6
is disadvantaged on three independent counts: fewer labels, a different assay
readout (label-free Echo-MS parent depletion rather than the fluorogenic Vivid
panel used for the other three), and — per the challenge documentation — no
representation in the analog-expansion step that built the test set, since the
2D6 assay was still in development. Its test compounds are therefore further
from its training distribution than any other isoform's. The same ordering
appears in the TDI track, where CYP2D6 AUC (0.605) is near chance while CYP3A4
reaches 0.809.
Threshold tuning dominated model tuning for MCC. Moving the CYP3A4 cutoff
from 0.5 to 0.23 raised MCC from 0.292 to 0.404; CYP2D6 went from 0.049 to
0.141. At a ~21% positive rate the default cutoff is badly miscalibrated for a
metric that balances both classes. No change to features or hyperparameters
came close to this effect size.
A metric-aware adjustment we tried and rejected. Since ST-RAE assigns zero
error anywhere inside the credible interval, and those intervals widen
substantially below pIC50 4, we hypothesized that shrinking low predictions
toward the empirical interval centre would be free insurance. We swept the
shrinkage strength on scaffold-OOF predictions:
strength	0.0	0.3	0.5	0.7	1.0
macro ST-RAE	0.446	0.452	0.458	0.465	0.477
It degrades the score monotonically, and the effect is strongest on CYP3A4
(0.313 → 0.410), the best-fit isoform. The explanation is that the model's
low-range predictions already fall inside the wide intervals and score zero;
pulling them toward a single global centre moves them back out. The idea was
sound in principle and wrong in fact. Shrinkage is disabled in the submitted
model (`SHRINK_STRENGTH = 0.0`).

7. Limitations and future work
The sparse-train / dense-test mismatch is unexploited. Four independent
single-task models discard all cross-isoform signal. A multitask model with a
shared molecular encoder and four heads, trained with masked loss over missing
labels, is the natural next step — the 1,309 compounds with ≥2 labels would
supply the coupling signal.
The 17,504-measurement single-concentration screening set was unused. It
covers 4,376 compounds against all four enzymes and is the largest weakly
labeled resource in the challenge pack. Pretraining on it, then fine-tuning on
the DRC labels, is the most promising unexplored direction here.
Credible-interval widths were used only for evaluation, not training. They
are per-compound uncertainty estimates and could weight the training loss
directly, downweighting poorly determined labels.
No ensembling, no hyperparameter search, no conformer or 3D features.
No uncertainty quantification on predictions.
