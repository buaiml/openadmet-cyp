OpenADMET CYP inhibition: ensemble methods and development results

Overview

We developed a structure-based ensemble to predict direct-inhibition pIC50 for CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2D6 and CYP3A4. The final recipe combines kernel support-vector regression, gradient-boosted trees, multitask neural networks and adapted MoLFormer models, with endpoint-specific weights.

The selected recipe achieved a macro ST-RAE of 0.6680464094 on a repeatedly used development-selection set. After selection, its 20 components were freshly fitted on 4,150 primary training structures to produce the final 750-molecule prediction file. The development score describes the earlier 3,437-row training run; the 4,150-row refit has no independently measured held-out score.

Data and evaluation design

The OpenADMET challenge dataset contains 4,905 direct-inhibition training structures and 750 blinded test structures. We divided the training set into three fixed partitions:

Partition Structures Chemical groups Use
Training, FIT 3,437 3,180 Model fitting and grouped cross-fitting
Selection 713 649 Repeated model and ensemble selection
Reserved assessment 755 700 Held apart from training and selection; outcomes remained sealed

Grouping used parent cleanup, uncharging, canonical tautomer normalization and stereo-collapsed connectivity, followed by Murcko scaffolds with a parent-identity fallback for acyclic compounds. Parent and scaffold groups were kept within a single partition. Prediction features used their own documented molecular representations, including chirality-aware channels.

Four grouped FIT folds contained 878, 841, 887 and 831 structures. Cross-fitting recomputed preprocessing and supervised-source exclusions within the permitted training identities. Missing targets were masked throughout.

The final primary training set combined FIT and selection, giving 4,150 structures. It contained 1,213, 1,090, 1,253 and 1,959 finite target-and-interval triplets for CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2D6 and CYP3A4, respectively. The reserved 755 outcomes remained excluded. Official blind outcomes were unavailable.

Endpoint and scoring metric

The outputs are operational pIC50 values under the challenge's direct-inhibition assay conditions. Their interpretation follows the official assay methods, rather than treating them as equilibrium binding constants or clinical interaction-risk estimates.

For a prediction p and experimental fitted interval [l, u], interval error is the distance from p to that interval:

d(p, [l, u]) = max(l - p, 0) + max(p - u, 0)

For each endpoint, ST-RAE divides the sum of prediction interval errors by the sum of interval errors from a constant predictor equal to the mean of the eligible evaluation point estimates. The macro score is the unweighted mean of the four endpoint ratios. Both the numerator and denominator use interval distance. Evaluator behavior was checked against the official scoring implementation.

We report both the full-sample ratio and the official-style mean over 1,000 endpoint-wise row-bootstrap replicates. Bootstrap standard deviations describe variability under that resampling procedure; they are neither confidence intervals nor corrections for repeated model selection.

Molecular representations

The main feature views combine four blocks:

  • G: chirality-aware Morgan fingerprints: radius-2 binary fingerprints with 2,048 components, radius-3 count fingerprints with 4,096 components, and feature-invariant radius-2 count fingerprints with 2,048 components.
  • E: 768-dimensional embeddings from the frozen public MoLFormer masked-language model.
  • P: RDKit physicochemical and topological descriptors.
  • I: 16 explicit charge, stereocenter, isotope, structural-count and graph-distance summaries.

GEPI uses all four blocks; EPI omits the graph fingerprints. Missing-value imputation, scaling and clipping were fitted on the applicable training partition and saved with each model. Kernel models combined generalized-Tanimoto fingerprint kernels with physical-descriptor or embedding RBF kernels according to the fixed component configuration.

Selected components also included bounded ETKDGv3 conformer summaries, geometry availability/status features and Dimorphite-DL rule-state summaries. The conformer procedure generated up to three unweighted conformers; unsupported stereochemistry or failed generation produced missing geometry with explicit flags. Rule-state features used a hypothetical pH sensitivity range of 6.4–8.4, rather than an assumed assay pH. Generated states and conformers were computational features, without inferred thermodynamic populations or binding poses.

The pretrained encoder was IBM MoLFormer-XL-both-10pct, revision 361063d0ad524ef77cf39b08469f6be770dc550f. Its documented pretraining is self-supervised on molecular structures; complete unlabeled overlap with challenge compounds is unknown. MoLFormer inputs used nonisomeric canonical SMILES, while separate chiral features retained stereochemical information. Inputs beyond the supported 202-token domain were rejected rather than truncated. All official challenge structures were covered.

Estimators and training

The final recipe contains four SVR components, five LightGBM components, four masked multitask neural components, two MoLFormer adaptation components and five ChEMBL-context components.

SVR and LightGBM. Finite target intervals were represented by their two endpoints, each carrying half weight. For an L1 objective, this gives interval distance plus a prediction-independent constant. SVR used precomputed kernels and epsilon = 0. The selected C values were 1 or 10. LightGBM used an L1 objective, 700 trees, 15 leaves, learning rate 0.03, minimum child count 20, feature fraction 0.8, L2 regularization 2 and seed 20261102. Production fitting used two threads.

Multitask networks. Shared networks used hidden layers of 256 and 128 units, SiLU activations and dropout of 0.2. They optimized masked, task-normalized absolute interval distance with AdamW, learning rate 0.001, weight decay 0.01 and batches of 128. Target means and normalizers came from the fitting data. The selected auxiliary model added separate standardized reporter-signal heads with a Huber-loss coefficient of 0.2. Selected checkpoints ranged from 50 to 200 epochs.

MoLFormer adaptation. Two components adapted the final encoder block, final normalization and prediction head from the pinned public checkpoint. Earlier encoder layers remained frozen. Encoder and head learning rates were 10^-5 and 0.001, respectively, with weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping at 1 and batch size 8. The selected checkpoints were epochs 2 and 4.

ChEMBL-context networks. These used separate official and external assay outputs, retaining assay context and censoring direction. The shared 256/128-unit architecture used dropout 0.2 and AdamW at 0.001 with weight decay 0.01. V3 added rank-8 context interactions with shrinkage 0.01, local batches of 128 and external batches of 256. Selected V2 components used external weight 0.3; selected V3 components included two zero-external-loss controls and one component with external weight 0.3.

Auxiliary supervision and provenance

The official auxiliary signals were TDI-arm reporter-signal contrasts at nominal 50 µM, recorded as 49.5049505 µM in the CSV. Training used 11,892 eligible observations across 2,973 FIT identities, with separate output heads and held-identity exclusions. Reduced signal can reflect both reversible and time-dependent effects. These measurements remained auxiliary training targets, with no conversion to enzyme-survival fractions or query-time assay features.

Native human CYP measurements from ChEMBL provided two fixed external pools:

  • V2: 78 observations from 78 molecule IDs and 77 normalized parents across ten assay contexts, comprising 56 exact values and 22 one-sided bounds.
  • V3: 5,304 observations from 3,474 molecule IDs and 3,366 normalized parents. CYP1A2 contributed 2,334 observations, including 998 exact and 1,336 one-sided values; CYP2D6 contributed 2,970, including 1,347 exact and 1,623 one-sided values.

External assays retained their own identities and scales. IC50 censoring was converted with the appropriate direction into pIC50 bounds. Selection, reserved-assessment and blind identities, normalized parents and scaffolds were excluded before supervised use; cross-fitting applied additional fold exclusions. Veith-origin and uncertain held-source contexts were excluded. One overlength external molecule, representing two observations, was removed through a uniform pre-outcome tokenizer-domain check.

The final refit kept these external pools and the original FIT-only auxiliary pool unchanged. Public-source provenance, assay/document identifiers, exclusions and source-specific weights were retained with the experiment records.

Development results and limitations

The initial search registered 119 base candidates or checkpoints, followed by additional controlled experiments. Components and blend weights were repeatedly selected on the same 713 structures. The resulting scores therefore carry selection optimism.

The official-style bootstrap macro mean was 0.6724705803, with sample standard deviation 0.0252024118. On the same selection cohort, an earlier ensemble scored 0.6726637641 by the full-sample calculation and 0.6771186220 by bootstrap averaging.

Further graph, learned-3D, CheMeleon, Emax, calibration, interaction and tabular experiments failed their adoption criteria and were excluded from this production recipe. The apparent D6 selection benefit of V3 transfer also failed to replicate in its four-fold FIT comparison. These results limit claims about the added information supplied by individual model families.

Training resubstitution macro ST-RAE was 0.056018, compared with 0.668046 on selection, showing a substantial generalization gap. Selection ranking was weakest for CYP1A2 and CYP2D6. Unlabeled pretraining overlap, sparse target measurements, heterogeneous external assays and repeated selection remain relevant limitations. The scaffold-grouped development task also differs from the challenge's analogue-enriched blind panel, preventing direct numerical comparison with other teams' official scores.

Production refit and reproducibility

After fixing the recipe, all 20 components were freshly trained on the 4,150-row FIT-plus-selection set. Component settings, seeds, stopping epochs and ensemble weights were unchanged. The adapted transformer restarted from the public MoLFormer checkpoint, and fitted preprocessing was recomputed from permitted production data. The former selection rows consequently became training observations rather than a new validation set.

Sequential CPU training completed in 1,259.25 seconds, about 20.99 minutes, with peak child memory of approximately 1,089 MiB. Saved-model reloads matched immediate fitted predictions bitwise. Fresh structure-based inference took 144.54 seconds at approximately 996 MiB peak memory. Restored-archive cold inference took 65.36 seconds at approximately 882 MiB.

Cached-feature, fresh-feature and restored-package inference produced byte-identical CSVs. Independent weighted reconstruction also matched all 3,000 endpoint predictions exactly. The output preserved all 750 official identifiers, raw SMILES and row order, and passed the pinned official submission validator.

The inference environment was Linux x86_64 with Python 3.12, tested with glibc 2.41. The production archive is a versioned model delta requiring the unchanged base runtime, public encoder and microstate assets listed in its dependency manifest. It contains the new weights rather than reusing the earlier fitted models.
