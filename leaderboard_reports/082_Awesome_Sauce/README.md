# OpenADMET CYP Blind Challenge — Method Report

**Tracks entered:** Direct Inhibition (regression) · Time-Dependent Inhibition (classification) · Structure Prediction

**Author:** `Sharma Nandini Surendra` — `Indian Institute of Technology (BHU), Varanasi`

---

## 1. Summary

Every submission was validated against the challenge's stated format
requirements before upload.

| Track | Approach | Headline result |
|---|---|---|
| Direct inhibition (regression) | Per-isoform LightGBM, L1 blended with an interval-hinge loss matched to the metric, plus Tanimoto-kNN | Macro ST-RAE **0.7026** (nested 0.7042) |
| TDI (classification) | ECFP4 + descriptors + bioactivation SMARTS; LightGBM blended with Tanimoto-kNN and a shift-regression member; MCC-optimal threshold | Nested CV MCC **0.395** (CYP3A4), **0.167** (CYP2D6) |
| Structure Prediction | Ensemble rigid-receptor AutoDock Vina docking into four CYP3A4 crystal templates, heme retained, poses re-ranked on heme proximity | 20/20 pass PoseBusters; all 20 within 5.6 Å of the heme iron |

**Scope and framing.** All three tracks were entered. They are usefully
complementary rather than three variants of one method: regression and TDI are
statistical-learning problems sharing a featurisation, while the structure track
is a physics-based structural problem requiring entirely different tooling, so
together they span both halves of a computational-chemistry workflow.

The sections are ordered as the work was done — TDI (§3) and structure (§4)
first, regression (§5) last — rather than in track order, because each stage
informed the next.

Three specific questions motivated the work, and the interesting part is that
two of the three answers were *no*:

1. **TDI.** The label is generated by a known deterministic rule applied to two
   measured assay arms. Can that generative structure be exploited to beat a
   classifier trained on the bare boolean? **Largely no** — the underlying shift
   is real signal but near-invisible to these features (§3.4). The genuine gain
   came instead from fixing an over-optimistic validation protocol (§3.5).
2. **Structure.** Can a classical rigid-receptor docking baseline clear the
   challenge's physical-plausibility gate, given that failing PoseBusters
   forfeits the score entirely? **Yes, 20/20** — but passing it turned out to be
   necessary and not sufficient, and auditing beyond it exposed five poses in
   the wrong pocket (§4.2).
3. **Regression.** The metric scores zero inside a credible interval whose width
   varies ten-fold across compounds. Does training a loss function that matches
   that tolerance beat plain L1? **Only where the tolerance is reachable** — it
   wins on CYP3A4 (40% inactive) and loses on CYP2D6 (9% inactive) (§5.3).

## 3. TDI track

### 3.1 A structural property of the label

`is_TDI` is not an independently measured quantity. The FAQ's labelling rules
reproduce the shipped booleans for **100.000%** of labelled rows, because the
label is a hard threshold on two measured assay arms — direct-inhibition pIC50
`d` and TDI-arm pIC50 `s`:

- **positive** if `d > 4` and `s − d > 0.301` (a >2-fold shift)
- **positive** if `d ≤ 4` and `s > 4.301` (inferred positive)
- **negative** otherwise

This was verified rather than assumed (`explore_tdi.py`), and it motivated the
central experiment below.

### 3.2 Features

Three blocks (`cyp_features.py`):

1. **ECFP4**, Morgan radius 2, 2048 bits.
2. **29 physicochemical descriptors** — LogP, TPSA, MolWt, aromatic/aliphatic
   ring counts, FractionCSP3, basic-nitrogen count, etc. CYP inhibition tracks
   lipophilicity and aromatic character strongly, which is mechanistically
   reasonable given the large hydrophobic heme-adjacent pockets.
3. **39 SMARTS counts for bioactivation motifs** — the only block specific to
   TDI rather than inherited from the regression track. TDI is overwhelmingly
   mechanism-based: the enzyme turns the parent compound into a reactive species
   that alkylates the apoprotein or coordinates the heme iron as a
   metabolic-intermediate complex. That chemistry comes from a documented set of
   substructures — methylenedioxyphenyl and terminal alkynes (carbene and
   MI-complex formers), furans and thiophenes (reactive epoxides and S-oxides),
   catechols and *para*-aminophenols (quinone-imines), basic amines
   (N-dealkylation to nitroso), heme-coordinating azoles. Explicit counts are a
   more direct route than hoping a fingerprint rediscovers them from ~1.5k
   labelled examples.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** no ablation was run. The mechanistic argument for
block 3 is sound but its actual contribution is **unmeasured**.

### 3.3 Validation design

**Scaffold-grouped 5-fold CV** (`GroupKFold` on Bemis–Murcko scaffolds). The
blind test set was built by analog expansion around known hits, so random folds
would place near-duplicates of training compounds into validation and report a
score the leaderboard cannot reproduce. Scaffold grouping makes the reported
number worse and more honest.

### 3.4 The main experiment — and its negative result

**Hypothesis.** If the label is a threshold on a continuous shift, then a
classifier on the boolean discards magnitude: a 0.31 shift and a 1.50 shift are
identical training signal. Regressing the shift and re-applying the rules should
recover that information.

**It largely failed.** The shift is close to unpredictable from structure:

| endpoint | shift SD | regression RMSE | Spearman ρ |
|---|---|---|---|
| CYP2D6 | 0.408 | 0.426 | 0.113 |
| CYP3A4 | 0.364 | 0.364 | 0.250 |

RMSE at or above the target's own standard deviation means the regression does
not beat predicting the mean. Applying the rules to predicted values with no
tuning gives MCC 0.095 (CYP2D6) and 0.156 (CYP3A4) — far below the plain
classifier. The route survives only as a **decorrelated blend member** on
CYP3A4, worth about +0.035 MCC.

**Why the shift resists prediction.** The two arms are highly correlated
(*r* = 0.896 for CYP2D6, 0.947 for CYP3A4) and each spans about one log unit
(SD 0.92 and 1.09). Differencing them deliberately cancels that shared
component — which is precisely the part a fingerprint model predicts well,
because it is binding affinity — and leaves a residual with SD ≈ 0.4.

That residual is **not** measurement noise. The dataset's own per-compound
fitted standard deviations give a noise floor on the difference of only
0.095 (CYP2D6) and 0.120 (CYP3A4) log units, so the observed spread is 3–4×
larger than the assay can account for, implying a genuine compound-specific
signal of SD ≈ 0.40 and 0.34. The shift is a real, reproducible property that
varies between compounds. Our features simply cannot see it (ρ = 0.11–0.25).

This points to a **representation failure rather than an irreducible ceiling**,
which matters because the two have different remedies. A >2-fold IC50 shift
requires catalytic turnover at a specific position, survival of the resulting
reactive intermediate, and capture by a nucleophile or the heme iron. That is a
property of the metabolic pathway and its transition states — *which* site is
oxidised and what becomes of the product — whereas ECFP4 encodes which
substructures are present, which is what governs binding. Two close analogs can
bind identically and be oxidised at different positions. The informative next
features are therefore ones that encode metabolic fate: site-of-metabolism
predictions, quantum descriptors such as bond-dissociation energies or Fukui
indices at candidate oxidation sites, and docking-derived distances from
oxidisable atoms to the heme iron.

### 3.5 Where the real gain came from: fixing the validation

The first version tuned three blend weights and a decision threshold on the
full out-of-fold vector. Nesting the selection exposed that as optimism:

| endpoint | tuned on all OOF | fully nested |
|---|---|---|
| CYP2D6 | 0.190 | **0.109** |
| CYP3A4 | 0.402 | **0.395** |

The CYP2D6 gap is the diagnostic, and the per-fold weights confirmed it —
jumping between `(0.1,0.7,0.2)`, `(0.6,0.4,0.0)` and `(0.0,0.9,0.1)` across
folds. The free search was fitting fold noise.

Replacing it with a small set of **fixed** candidate blends, each evaluated with
only the threshold fitted in-fold, both removed the optimism and scored better:

| endpoint | selected blend (clf / knn / rule) | nested MCC | classifier alone |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CYP2D6_is_TDI` | `clf+knn` — 0.5 / 0.5 / 0.0 | **0.167** | 0.124 |
| `CYP3A4_is_TDI` | `clf+rule` — 0.8 / 0.0 / 0.2 | **0.395** | 0.360 |

Independent corroboration: for CYP3A4 the free simplex search converged on
`(0.8, 0.0, 0.2)` in **all five folds** — exactly the fixed candidate. Two
different selection procedures agreeing is stronger evidence than either alone.

Standalone member performance (AUC):

| member | CYP2D6 | CYP3A4 |
|---|---|---|
| LightGBM classifier | 0.577 | 0.802 |
| Tanimoto kNN | **0.616** | 0.708 |
| shift + rules | 0.590 | 0.641 |

The kNN *beating* the GBM on CYP2D6 is informative in itself.

**What the kNN result implies.** Two things. First, 1,497 compounds spread over
1,450 distinct scaffolds is roughly **1.03 compounds per scaffold** — the
CYP2D6 set is almost entirely scaffold singletons. A gradient-boosted ensemble
is being asked to recover global, feature-additive rules from a dataset that
contains essentially no repeated chemotype, so there is no within-series trend
for it to learn.

Second, the kNN's advantage survives *scaffold-grouped* folds, which
deliberately place a compound's own scaffold-mates in the same fold. The signal
it exploits is therefore cross-scaffold fingerprint similarity, not memorised
series — weak but genuine local continuity in a space where no global rule was
recoverable.

The transferability implication is specific and uncomfortable: the CYP2D6 model
**interpolates between known compounds rather than encoding a mechanism**, so it
should be expected to work only near its training set and to degrade sharply on
novel chemotypes. That happens to be mildly favourable for this particular
leaderboard, since the test set was built by analog expansion around known hits
and so sits unusually close to the training data — but it means almost no
transferable insight into CYP2D6 TDI was obtained, and the score should not be
read as evidence of any.

### 3.6 Operating point and the main unresolved risk

The decision threshold is transferred from training to test by **rank
normalisation** (quantile transfer). The alternative — isotonic calibration to
absolute probability — was tested and lost in nested CV for both selected
configurations (CYP2D6 0.167 vs 0.154; CYP3A4 0.395 vs 0.367), so rank was kept.

**That comparison cannot fully settle the question, and this is the weakest
point in the track.** Nested CV measures everything on the *training*
distribution, where the two transfers largely agree by construction.

What rank transfer actually does is worth stating precisely, because it is easy
to describe wrongly. It does **not** reproduce the training prior on the test
set — it reproduces the **out-of-fold operating quantile**. For a single-member
configuration the test positive rate is forced to exactly `1 − threshold`,
independent of what the model believes about the test molecules, and for CYP3A4
that is almost exactly what happens: `1 − 0.6018 = 0.398` against an observed
0.396.

| endpoint | train prior | `1 − threshold` | predicted positive on test |
|---|---|---|---|
| `CYP2D6_is_TDI` | 21.6% | 13.5% | 8.9% (67/750) |
| `CYP3A4_is_TDI` | 21.3% | 39.8% | 39.6% (297/750) |

So the submission calls 39.6% of CYP3A4 test compounds positive **because that
is where MCC peaked on the out-of-fold ranks**, not because the model judges
39.6% of them to be time-dependent inhibitors — roughly double the 21.3%
training prior. (CYP2D6 deviates from `1 − threshold` because a two-member
blend of rank vectors is not itself uniform.)

The consequence is that the predicted positive *rate* carries no information
from the test molecules; only the predicted *ranking* does. That is a coherent
design — MCC depends on the full confusion matrix, so transferring the operating
point by quantile is a reasonable way to preserve it — but it means the model
cannot respond to a shifted test distribution even in principle. If the analog
expansion really did enrich for actives, a calibrated-probability threshold
would adapt and rank transfer cannot. Nested CV favours rank, and nested CV is
blind to exactly this failure mode.

CYP2D6 at 8.9% is additionally a conservative, high-precision operating point
(out-of-fold precision 0.47, recall 0.19) that MCC maximisation selected on a
weak model.

**The two thresholds are not equally trustworthy**, and the shape of the MCC
surface shows it. For CYP3A4 the optimum is broad — every threshold in
[0.587, 0.618] is within 0.01 MCC of the peak, and moving ±0.05 costs only
0.02–0.03 — so the operating point is stable. For CYP2D6 it is a narrow spike:
the peak is 0.187 but the near-optimal window is only 0.018 wide
([0.853, 0.871]), and ±0.05 away MCC falls to 0.145 and 0.130. Outside that
spike the surface is nearly flat at 0.10–0.13 across positive rates from 18% to
75%. On a model with AUC 0.61 that spike is most plausibly fitted noise.

It was nevertheless kept, because the nested estimate already prices this in:
re-selecting the threshold inside every fold and applying it out-of-fold still
yields 0.167, against 0.124 for the classifier alone. The nested protocol
measures the *procedure*, fragile threshold selection included, so the
comparison remains valid — but the realised CYP2D6 score should be expected to
vary considerably more than the CYP3A4 one.

### 3.7 Honest assessment

CYP3A4 works reasonably: nested MCC 0.395, AUC 0.80. **CYP2D6 is weak** — nested
MCC 0.167, AUC 0.61 — and should not be presented as a working model. With 1,497
labels across 1,450 distinct scaffolds and a shift that barely correlates with
structure, there is little learnable global signal.

## 4. Structure Prediction track

### 4.1 Approach

Ensemble rigid-receptor docking with **AutoDock Vina 1.2.7**, which the track
rules explicitly permit.

**Four CYP3A4 templates**, chosen to span the pocket's conformational range —
CYP3A4's cavity volume differs several-fold between apo and ritonavir-bound:

| PDB | resolution | co-crystal ligand | pocket state |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1TQN | 2.05 Å | — (apo) | compact |
| 6MA7 | 2.09 Å | TPF (drug-like) | medium |
| 2V0M | 2.80 Å | ketoconazole | partly expanded |
| 3NXU | 2.00 Å | ritonavir | highly expanded |

**Preparation** (`prep_receptors.py`): one protein chain retained per template,
chosen as the chain with the most protein atoms that also carries a heme;
**heme kept**; solvent and co-crystal ligand removed. The 24 Å docking box is
centred on the co-crystal ligand's own centroid — the most reliable definition
of the site, which is otherwise a large diffuse cavity. For apo 1TQN there is no
such ligand, so the box is centred 6 Å off the heme plane along the distal
normal, with the direction fixed by pointing *away* from the proximal cysteine
so the box lands in the pocket rather than inside the porphyrin.

**Ligands** (`dock_structures.py`): RDKit ETKDGv3 embedding, MMFF94s
minimisation, Meeko preparation. Each ligand docked into all four templates at
exhaustiveness 32. Complexes written as receptor + pose with the ligand as
residue **`LIG`** in its own chain, with CONECT records.

### 4.2 Pose selection: why best-score is the wrong criterion

The first version kept, per ligand, the single best-scoring pose across the four
templates — and auditing the result exposed a failure that every format and
physics check had missed. Five of twenty ligands had their closest heavy atom
**7–10 Å from the heme iron**: `OCNT-2395503` (9.13 Å), `OCNT-2395457`
(9.99 Å), `OCNT-2313300` (8.10 Å), `OCNT-2395328` (9.55 Å), `OCNT-2315379`
(7.46 Å). In a 24 Å box centred on the co-crystal ligand that is reachable — it
is the substrate access channel or a peripheral sub-pocket, not the catalytic
site.

For a CYP that is almost certainly wrong. These compounds were assayed as
CYP3A4 inhibitors, a competitive inhibitor must occupy the heme-distal pocket,
and a pose 9 Å from the iron cannot engage the catalytic machinery at all. Such
poses **pass PoseBusters perfectly** — physically clean, mechanistically
meaningless — and would score near zero on LDDT-PLI against a ground truth that
places the ligand above the heme. Passing the physics gate gave false comfort.

The fix was nearly free, because Vina already samples multiple poses per
receptor and the first version discarded all but the top one. Every pose from
every template is now kept and ranked on a **tiered** criterion: among poses
that reach the heme, take the best Vina score; fall back to looser tiers only if
none qualify.

| tier | min(ligand heavy atom → Fe) | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | ≤ 6.5 Å | catalytically competent |
| 2 | ≤ 9.0 Å | in the distal pocket |
| 3 | anything | flagged in the manifest |

6.5 Å is deliberately generous — typical site-of-metabolism distances are
4–5 Å — so it admits any pose that could plausibly present an atom to the iron
while excluding the access-channel poses. This **re-ranks rather than
re-samples**, so it cannot degrade the ligands that were already correct: their
top-scoring pose usually satisfies tier 1 and is reselected unchanged.

### 4.3 Results

All 20 ligands docked, **all 20 in tier 1**. 7 of 20 selections changed relative
to best-score ranking.

| | best-score (v1) | heme-aware (v2) |
|---|---|---|
| max ligand-to-Fe distance | 9.99 Å | **5.57 Å** |
| mean ligand-to-Fe distance | — | **4.00 Å** |
| mean Vina score | −8.89 | −8.83 |

The entire cost of putting every ligand in the catalytic site was **0.06
kcal/mol of mean Vina score** — a trade worth making, since Vina score is a
proxy and the metric is interface accuracy.

| validation gate | result |
|---|---|
| format — 20 files, correct names, `LIG` + `HEM` present, heavy atoms match SMILES | 20/20 |
| ligand within 6.5 Å of heme iron | 20/20 |
| **PoseBusters** — 22 checks, `config="dock"` | **20/20 pass** |

PoseBusters remains the gate that matters most — a failure is a guaranteed zero
rather than a low score — but as §4.2 shows, passing it is necessary and not
sufficient.

### 4.4 Limitations

- **Rigid receptor.** Four fixed crystal conformations cannot reproduce induced
  fit on a pocket the organisers themselves describe as notoriously plastic.
  The ground truth is cryo-EM — a different method from the X-ray templates.
- **Template still chosen by Vina score** (within the heme-proximity tier).
  Score picks the template that *scores* best, not the one closest to the true
  conformation, and inter-template spreads are often small (0.2–1.0 kcal/mol),
  so for several ligands the choice remains close to arbitrary. All candidate
  scores and Fe distances are retained in `docking_manifest.json` so the
  selection can be audited.
- **The 6.5 Å tier cutoff is a prior, not a measurement.** It encodes the
  expectation that these inhibitors bind above the heme. That is well founded
  for competitive CYP3A4 inhibitors, but CYP3A4 does have documented peripheral
  and allosteric sites, so if any of the 20 genuinely binds there, the filter
  has forced it into the wrong pocket. The tiering makes the assumption explicit
  and auditable rather than hidden.
- **Vina has no heme-coordination term.** This is a real physical omission for
  CYP substrates: azole nitrogens coordinating the heme iron is a dominant
  binding mode, imidazoles appear in at least four of these 20 ligands, and the
  scoring function cannot see it. Poses may be systematically misoriented for
  exactly the compounds where the interaction matters most.
- **No pose confidence estimate.** One pose per ligand, no uncertainty.
- **A co-folding model would likely do better.** Boltz-2 or Chai-1 handles
  induced fit and was the winning class of approach in the organisers' previous
  PXR challenge. It needs a GPU and was out of scope here.

**What to fix first.** Co-folding, because it is the only option that attacks
the root cause. The receptor here is frozen in four conformations borrowed from
other ligands, while CYP3A4's pocket reorganises around whatever it binds, so no
amount of sampling within a rigid template can recover induced fit. It also has
direct precedent: co-folding workflows were the winning class of approach in the
organisers' own PXR challenge. Running Boltz-2 or Chai-1 on a GPU is a bounded
piece of work, and because the submission format is a complex PDB, the rest of
this pipeline — `LIG` naming, the PoseBusters gate, packaging, verification —
is reusable unchanged.

Two cheaper fixes are worth doing first, since they cost almost nothing.
Template selection is currently by Vina score, which is close to arbitrary
wherever the four scores fall within ~0.2 kcal/mol of each other; selecting
instead by pocket-volume match to the ligand, or by consensus across templates,
is a few hours of work. And adding an explicit Fe-coordination restraint — or
simply rescoring poses that place an azole nitrogen within coordination distance
of the heme iron — would address a known blind spot in Vina's scoring function
that affects at least four of these 20 ligands.

## 5. Direct Inhibition track (regression)

Target: pIC50 for CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2D6, CYP3A4 across the 750 blind
compounds. Metric: macro-averaged Soft-Threshold RAE (lower is better).

### 5.1 The metric dictates the loss function

ST-RAE measures the distance from the prediction to the nearest bound of the
fitted dose-response credible interval, and is **zero anywhere inside that
interval**. The training file carries `conf_low`/`conf_high` for every labelled
row (100% coverage), and the widths are extremely uneven:

| isoform | median CI width, pIC50 < 4 | median CI width, pIC50 >= 5 | fraction < 4 |
|---|---|---|---|
| CYP1A2 | 2.09 | 0.24 | 16.4% |
| CYP2C9 | 1.21 | 0.26 | 20.2% |
| CYP2D6 | 2.59 | 0.22 | 8.6% |
| CYP3A4 | 2.15 | 0.14 | 40.4% |

An inactive compound can therefore be predicted anywhere across a ~2 log-unit
band for free, while a potent one must land within ~0.2. A plain L1 regressor
is blind to this: it spends identical effort pinning down an inactive's noisy
point estimate and a potent compound's sharp one, and most of the former earns
nothing.

So the loss was matched to the metric — an **interval-hinge objective**

```
L(p) = max(lo - p, 0) + max(p - hi, 0)
```

implemented as a LightGBM custom objective with gradient −1 / 0 / +1 and
constant hessian (the same device LightGBM's own L1 uses). Because LightGBM
zeroes the initial score for a custom objective, the training median is supplied
as an explicit `init_score` and added back at prediction time.

This **supersedes the shrinkage heuristic** tried for this track earlier, which
attacked the same asymmetry by pulling predictions toward an inactive floor and
tuned to approximately zero effect. Shrinkage was a scalar proxy applied after
the fact; the hinge loss puts the same information into the objective, per
compound, where the model can act on it.

### 5.2 Results

Out-of-fold ST-RAE under scaffold-grouped 5-fold CV, per member:

| isoform | L1 | hinge | kNN | selected blend | ST-RAE |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CYP1A2 | 0.8068 | 0.8452 | — | `l1` | **0.8068** |
| CYP2C9 | 0.6648 | 0.6677 | — | `l1+hinge` (0.5/0.5) | **0.6348** |
| CYP2D6 | 0.8894 | 0.9402 | 1.0846 | `l1+knn` (0.8/0.2) | **0.8837** |
| CYP3A4 | 0.5194 | 0.5067 | 1.0786 | `l1+hinge` (0.5/0.5) | **0.4850** |

**Macro-averaged ST-RAE: 0.7026** (nested estimate 0.7042), against 0.7201 for
L1 alone and 0.7399 for the hinge alone. All endpoints score below 1.0, i.e.
every model beats the mean predictor.

Secondary metrics for the selected blends: MAE 0.47–0.65, R² 0.16–0.55,
Spearman ρ 0.42–0.76. The modest R² is expected — the dynamic range is only
about one log unit, so a model doing real work still looks unimpressive.

### 5.3 The hypothesis was half right, and the data says exactly where

The hinge loss **beats L1 only on CYP3A4** (0.5067 vs 0.5194) and loses
elsewhere, badly on CYP2D6 (0.9402 vs 0.8894). The pattern is clean and
mechanistically sensible: the hinge's advantage comes entirely from slack on
wide inactive intervals, so it needs inactives to exploit. CYP3A4 is 40.4%
inactive; CYP2D6 is 8.6%. With few inactives there is little free slack and the
hinge's zero-gradient region simply discards usable gradient signal that L1 uses.

So the right conclusion is not "match the loss to the metric" but "match the
loss to the metric *when the metric's tolerance is actually reachable*". The
blend is what wins, because L1 and the hinge make different errors.

**Expectation for the blind set.** This gain is unlikely to transfer at full
strength, and the reason is worth stating in advance rather than after the
leaderboard. The hinge's entire advantage is slack on wide intervals, and wide
intervals belong to inactives. The test set was built by analog expansion around
top hits, so it should be *more* active-rich than CYP3A4's 40%-inactive training
distribution — which means less free slack, and a blend weight (0.5/0.5) fitted
on a distribution the test set does not share. Since potent compounds carry
intervals of only ~0.14–0.26 log units, test ST-RAE will be dominated by exactly
the regime where L1 is the stronger member. The honest prediction is therefore
that CYP3A4's realised ST-RAE will sit worse than its 0.4850 cross-validated
value, and that a pure-L1 model would have been the safer choice there had the
test distribution been known.

### 5.4 Limitations

- **Single-task models.** The four isoforms genuinely correlate and a multi-task
  model would let the sparser endpoints (CYP2C9, n=1285) borrow representation
  from CYP3A4 (n=2335). This is the most likely source of further gain and was
  dropped for time, not because it is wrong.
- **The ST-RAE denominator convention is inferred** from the challenge prose,
  not the official scorer. It is a constant per endpoint, so model selection and
  ranking here are unaffected, but the absolute values should not be read as
  leaderboard predictions.
- **The single-concentration screening file is unused** (17,504 measurements
  over 4,376 compounds). It covers far more chemical space at coarser
  resolution and is the obvious pretraining signal.
- **No uncertainty estimates**, despite a metric built around credible
  intervals. Predicting an interval rather than a point is the natural
  extension.
