## 2. The task and the metric

**Task.** Predict pIC50 against four CYP isoforms for 750 blinded compounds.

**Metric.** ST-RAE (soft-threshold relative absolute error), averaged over the four isoforms, plus
MAE, R², Spearman ρ and Kendall τ. Per isoform,

    ST-RAE = Σ softerr(pred) / Σ softerr(mean(y_true)),
    softerr(p) = max(p − hi, 0) + max(lo − p, 0)

where `[lo, hi]` is the dose-response curve's 95% credible band. A prediction inside the band costs
nothing, and the denominator is the same error for a constant predictor at the test-label mean.
Two properties, both measured on the leaderboard, shaped the whole project:

- The overall row is the plain mean of the four isoform rows (checked to four decimals on the
  board rows of 2026-10-22, 10-29 and 11-02), so each isoform can be improved independently.
- ST-RAE is far more level-sensitive than MAE. A shift of +0.172 on CYP3A4 moved ST-RAE by −5.5%,
  R² by +1.8% and MAE by −0.27%. On a well-predicted isoform a 1% MAE change corresponded to a
  17% ST-RAE change (CYP2C9, window-arm row).

**Spearman and Kendall are invariant to any monotone map of a column.** That makes them an
end-to-end integrity check: after a constant shift or a permutation of existing values the board's
ρ and τ must come back identical. They did, to four decimals, for the CYP2D6 shift and the CYP3A4 shift.

## 3. Data

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Training compounds | 4,905 |
| Dose-response pIC50 labels | 1,412 (CYP1A2) / 1,285 (CYP2C9) / 1,493 (CYP2D6) / 2,335 (CYP3A4) |
| Single-concentration screen | 4,375 of the 4,905 compounds, all four isoforms, at one concentration (≈ 49.5 µM, pIC50 4.305). The other 530 hold a CYP3A4 pIC50 and no screen row. |
| Blinded test set | 750 compounds, built from the top ≈ 25 hits per isoform plus ≈ 10 purchased chemisimilars per hit; maximum Tanimoto similarity to training ≈ 0.50–0.55 |

**The labels are missing-not-at-random.** Only compounds that hit in the screen were
dose-responded, so a model trained on the labels alone sees only the potent edge and predicts
that edge for everything. Cross-validation on labelled compounds cannot detect this. The blinded
set is shifted towards weaker compounds, so the failure appears only on the leaderboard (internal
CV R² 0.47 on CYP2D6 against a board R² of −0.91 for the same model).

**What one screen readout is worth** (`notebooks/cyp_screen_window.py`). Compounds that carry both a
screen readout and a pIC50 let the readout be calibrated. With `r = 2^log2FC` (activity remaining),
pIC50 is strongly monotone in `r` (Spearman −0.862 / −0.896 / −0.828 / −0.936 for CYP1A2 / 2C9 /
2D6 / 3A4). The "no inhibition" bin (`r ≥ 0.9`, n = 303 pooled) has median pIC50 **2.21**, with all
303 below 4.305. So the 49.5 µM bound is correct as a *ceiling* and wrong by about 2 log units as a
*location*. CYP2D6 has no compound in that bin, so the calibration is pooled across isoforms (1,929
labelled compounds against 200 from CYP2D6 alone, over the readout range the unpromoted CYP2D6
compounds occupy). Applied to the 2,882 unpromoted CYP2D6 compounds, the calibrated window is
centred at 2.46 and 94.9% of windows lie entirely below 4.305.

Two representations reach the submitted ordering. A third reaches the *values* of the CYP2C9 and

**The extended descriptor vector.** One row per compound, read from
`data/compounds/compounds_master.csv` (computed once and cached; one shared loader,

signal here (no basic amine, no SMARTCyp site). Compounds failing the descriptor NaN-rate audit are

The vector's width has moved, and the 129-column FRED/Chemgauss4 docking block is the reason to read

## 4. The model

### 4.1 Members

Seven members per isoform, from two feature spaces (§3.1) and two model families (`notebooks/cyp_stacker.py`):

- **`tabpfn_monroe`** — **Monroe**, a 720-dimensional graph-transformer embedding pretrained on
  quantum-geometry and bioassay data, used *frozen* (never fine-tuned here), fed to **TabPFN v3.5**
  (one 8-estimator ensemble, PCA explained-variance 1.0, softmax temperature 0.9).
- **`lgbm_morgan`** — LightGBM on Morgan count fingerprints (radius 2, 2,048 bits).

Four label treatments, which are the "arms":

| Arm | Labels the model is trained on |
|---|---|
| `natural` | dose-response pIC50 only (the potent edge) |
| `floor` | `natural` + each unpromoted compound labelled at 4.305 |
| `window` | `natural` + each unpromoted compound labelled at the *calibrated window centre* (§3) |
| `interval` (LightGBM only) | as `window`, but with a two-sided interval loss that charges nothing inside the window |

`tabpfn_monroe` runs `natural`, `floor` and `window`; `lgbm_morgan` runs all four, giving seven
members. Final predictions for the blinded set refit every member on the challenge compounds only,
every compound that carries a label or a screen readout for that isoform: 4,375 for CYP1A2, CYP2C9
and CYP2D6 (1,412 / 1,285 / 1,493 promoted plus 2,963 / 3,090 / 2,882 window-labelled) and 4,905
for CYP3A4 (2,335 promoted plus 2,570 window-labelled). No public rows enter any member's training (but see §4.3 for the level). The TabPFN
refit uses a single seed (0). A CPU rerun of
the CYP2D6 refit agreed with the GPU run at Spearman 0.997 (window) / 0.995 (natural).

### 4.2 The combiner

For each isoform, non-negative least squares (NNLS) is fitted on the members' **held-out**
predictions from 5 Murcko-scaffold GroupKFold folds × 3 repeats, so no member's in-sample output
reaches the fit. Non-negativity matters: an unconstrained ridge combiner bought its fit with
cancelling coefficients down to −0.525 (L1 norm 1.25–1.86 against NNLS's 1.00–1.07), the same
failure mode as the affine recalibrations that lost on the board (§5.3). Weights in the shipped file:

| Isoform | Weights (sum) |
|---|---|
| CYP1A2 | tabpfn window 0.4217, tabpfn natural 0.3779, lgbm floor 0.0894, lgbm window 0.0744, tabpfn floor 0.0705, lgbm interval 0.0392 (1.0732) |
| CYP2C9 | tabpfn window 0.4340, tabpfn natural 0.3488, lgbm window 0.1811, tabpfn floor 0.0423, lgbm floor 0.0235 (1.0296) |
| CYP2D6 | not stacked |
| CYP3A4 | tabpfn window 0.6352, tabpfn natural 0.2755, lgbm window 0.0569, lgbm natural 0.0370 (1.0046) |

### 4.3 Placement: keep the board-measured level, change only the order

The stack is not shipped as a fitted value. Each column is `quantile_map(stack_order, scored_level)`:
the stack's *ordering* carrying the values of a column the leaderboard had already scored. Mean,
standard deviation and every order statistic are therefore bit-identical to that earlier column
(verified on the shipped file: the sorted values of CYP1A2, CYP2C9 and CYP2D6 equal the best-of
file's, and CYP3A4's equal them plus 0.172). Spearman against the scored columns is 0.989 / 0.952 /
1.000 / 0.960.

The reason is that the held-out surface scores *promoted* compounds, where the natural model has no
bias to remove, so it cannot rate a level. The blinded set carries board-solved biases of about
0.70 / 0.19 / 1.55 / 0.20 (CYP1A2 / 2C9 / 2D6 / 3A4) in the natural column. That surface had
already misjudged a level once: it rated the `window` arm's CYP3A4 level as fine, and the board
scored it 0.5654 → 0.6819. Holding the level at a measured value is the one choice the surface
cannot get wrong.

**Where the borrowed values come from, and what that means for public data.** CYP1A2 and CYP2D6
take their values from the `window` arm of the 2026-10-22 refit, which is challenge-only. CYP2C9
and CYP3A4 take theirs from the 2026-10-14 file `submission_history/20261014_095010_my_activity_submission_monroe.csv`,
which was written by `activity_prediction_monroe.ipynb`, not by the shard or refit scripts. That
notebook's final fit trains TabPFN on `model_df` (the notebook version of 2026-10-13, the one with
the `monroe+ext` configuration this run reports), which holds the challenge compounds *plus the
curated public-assay rows*, with Monroe embeddings and the extended descriptors as features. The run's
own training population is not recorded in its manifest row, so this is read from the notebook code.
The stack takes only the *values* of that column and reassigns them by its own ordering. Public
data therefore shapes the level and spread of those two columns and not their order. Whether it
contributes to the board-solved offsets of that column (+0.19 / +0.20) is untested.

### 4.4 Two isoforms handled differently

- **CYP2D6 is not stacked.** On CYP1A2, CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 the surface reproduced the board's
  `window`-over-`natural` Spearman gain to three decimals (surface +0.0106 / +0.0211 / +0.0129
  against board +0.0106 / +0.0211 / +0.0128). On CYP2D6 it showed +0.0042 where the board measured **+0.1331**, a 32-fold
  understatement, because the promoted training rows contain none of the weak compounds the window
  labels help with. A stack fitted on that view starves the window arms (weight 0.16) and reorders
  742 of 750 compounds, so CYP2D6 keeps the `window` arm.
- **CYP3A4 is shifted by +0.172.** The first CYP3A4 stack lost (+0.0040 ST-RAE where −0.0237 was
  predicted). Its members were then found to have been trained on 1,805 of the isoform's 2,335
  labels (§5.2) and were refit on all of them. A constant +0.172 was added on top and scored a win.
  The rationale for that specific value is **not recorded**. An after-the-fact solve from the two
  board rows puts the R²-optimal shift at +0.112 to +0.139, so 0.172 overshoots on R², and the
  ST-RAE optimum is not solvable (§7).

### 5.2 What each step taught

1. **Rows 1–2: a location error can dominate everything.** Predicted CYP2D6 values were centred at
   4.70 with spread 0.42 against training labels with spread 0.92, for a test set whose label mean
   the board solves to roughly 3.0–3.3. The offset was solved from the board's four published scalars (ST-RAE, MAE, R², ρ) and the
   column's own mean and spread. Two submissions differing by a known constant then let the true
   offset be bracketed with no distributional assumption at +1.39 to +1.73, so −1.45 under-shot; R²
   rose from −0.913 to +0.2524. ρ and τ were identical to four decimals, confirming only the
   location had moved.
2. **Row 3: calibrated interval labels fix the weak end but not the level on already-centred
   isoforms.** The arm lowers every isoform by about the natural column's bias, whether or not that
   bias is there to remove. It won where the natural bias was large (CYP1A2 0.70, CYP2D6 1.55) and
   lost where it was small (CYP2C9 0.19, CYP3A4 0.20). The ordering improved on three of four
   isoforms (ρ +0.0139 / +0.0630 / +0.1331 for CYP1A2 / 2C9 / 2D6; CYP3A4 unchanged), which is what
   made the later rank transfer worth trying. A warning (CYP2C9's window mean sat 0.17 below its
   board-implied label mean) was in the pre-submission reading and was recorded as a caveat rather
   than acted on.
3. **Row 4: columns can be chosen independently**, because the macro-average has no cross-isoform term.
4. **Row 5: stacking improved ordering on every stacked isoform** (board ρ 0.8116 / 0.8366 / 0.7720,
   each better than both ingredient orderings the board had scored), but ST-RAE disagreed with MAE
   on CYP3A4: MAE improved by 0.0061 while ST-RAE worsened by 0.0040, because the permutation
   pushed some compounds further outside their credible bands.
5. **Row 6: the CYP3A4 data bug.** The window arm had been training CYP3A4 on 1,805 of 2,335 labels:
   530 compounds with a dose-response label but no screen row were dropped by a join. The code was
   fixed on 2026-10-22, but no artifact on disk reflected the fix until 2026-10-31. Retraining on all
   labels gained 0.027 of ordering (ρ 0.7720 → 0.7988) and was the whole macro move of that row.
6. **Row 7: CYP3A4 location.** After the fix, the 0.5435 → 0.5134 gain on CYP3A4 came from a
   constant. Re-solving from the two board rows shows the 10-31 column was too low by 0.112 to 0.139
   (label mean about 4.82–4.85), and that placement can add at most about +0.034 R² more (shift and
   spread together). The remaining gap to the leading entries is ordering (information), not level.

In-distribution resampling cannot see the test set's shift. The Monroe notebook's nested test showed the same pattern (worse on 3 of 4 isoforms). |
| **Weak-end arms** (`floor`, `tobit`, `hurdle`, `l2proxy`, ±public inactives) for CYP2D6 | label-backed "shift" miniature of the test shift, LightGBM and TabPFN | cut weak-band MAE by 0.28–0.36 but anchored at 4.305, so 73–79% of known-unpromoted compounds were still predicted above the bound (TabPFN, −1.2 cut); superseded by calibrated windows. Ranking on the weak band did not improve. |
| **Atom-JEPA** (3D equivariant encoder, fine-tuned multitask) | pinned-490 ensemble, embedding-only | mean R² 0.366, on par with the best GNN, no gain over it; not shipped |
| **CheMeleon / ChemBERTa-2 embeddings, 1,402-column extended descriptors** | paired CV; embeddings 96% / 74% linearly predictable from existing descriptors | paired CV gains with LightGBM on three of four isoforms; in the 2026-11-03 TabPFN sweep, extended descriptors on top of Monroe changed R² by −0.0035 to +0.0039 (significant only on CYP2D6, and negative there), so the shipped members are Monroe-only |
| **Window-label V1 surface** (promoted held-out compounds only) | 8 shards × 15 (rep, fold) units | rated the `window` arm worse than `natural` on CYP1A2 and CYP2D6 by large margins, the opposite of the board; cannot represent the blinded set's population |
| **Physics / structure features** (docking descriptors, heme geometry, MACE energies) | built for Tier 1 | none reached a shipped model; the 118-column docking descriptor file had no model fit at the time of the 2026-10-22 audit |
| **Public-assay weight sweep** | rows 14–16 | invalid (a prediction-time bug made every arm's metric near random); re-run as rows 25–27 with the extended descriptors and trust weighting added at the same time, mean R² 0.161–0.179 against 0.270–0.279 for the earlier rows without them. The drop was not isolated to either change. |

### 5.4 What the offline estimates predicted, against the board

| Step | Offline estimate | Board result |
|---|---|---|
| CYP2D6 −1.45 shift | R² ≈ +0.14 (both anchors) | R² **+0.2524** (conservative) |
| `window` arm, all isoforms | win on 4 of 4 (15/15 units on all 8 shards) | win on 2, loss on 2 (level, not ordering) |
| Stack ordering, ST-RAE change | −0.0058 / −0.0223 / −0.0237 (CYP1A2 / 2C9 / 3A4) | −0.0081 / −0.0733 / **+0.0040** |
| CYP3A4 on all 2,335 labels | direction only | −0.0259 (0.5694 → 0.5435), ρ +0.027 |

The offline surface was reliable for **ordering** (it matched the board's Spearman gains to three
decimals on three isoforms) and unreliable for **level** (CYP3A4 twice, CYP2D6 once). That
asymmetry is the reason for the placement rule in §4.3.

## 7. Limitations

1. **The board score is partly tuned to the board.** Four choices used leaderboard feedback on the
   same 750 compounds: the CYP2D6 offset (solved from board scalars), the per-isoform best-of, the
   decision to hold CYP2D6 out of the stack, and the CYP3A4 constant. The 0.5159 should be read as
   an in-sample figure for those choices. The ordering gains (ρ) are the more trustworthy part, as
   they are level-invariant and were predicted offline.
2. **The +0.172 is not justified by anything recorded when it was chosen.** The post-hoc solve
   places the R² optimum at +0.112 to +0.139, so it overshoots there. Whether it is better or worse
   than the optimum on ST-RAE is untested.
3. **The shipped model omits data and features the project built.** No public-assay compounds (the
   curated 9,434-compound table is used in the GNN and LightGBM notebooks only), no extended
   descriptors, no auxiliary heads, no GNN. The 2026-10-22 audit (`REPO_AUDIT.md`) lists this as the
   first of its four headline findings. The project's own design trains public compounds at equal
   weight to challenge compounds; no stack member uses them, and they reach CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 only
through the level source (item 4).
4. **Level sources are older files, and one was trained with public data.** CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 take
   their level from the natural column of the 2026-10-14 Monroe-notebook file. That notebook
   trains on challenge plus public rows with extended descriptors (§4.3). Its descriptor vector
   still held the split-confounded `nn_*` columns (§3.1), and the file carries a near-identity
   affine calibration (slopes 1.045 / 0.993). CYP1A2 and CYP2D6 take theirs from the
   2026-10-22 window refit, which is challenge-only. The link between the 2026-10-15 scored file
   and its history copy was inferred from timing and later supported by the files' content.
5. **The rank map has no logged board row.** The CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 deltas of row 5 are therefore
   measured against the best-of and may be partly the rank map's.
6. **CYP2D6 depends on a pooled calibration.** No CYP2D6 compound lies in the screen-readout range
   its unpromoted compounds occupy, so its weak-end window rests on the other three isoforms'
   labels. Its ρ (0.536) is the lowest of the four.
7. **Single TabPFN seed, one refit per member.** Seed-to-seed noise was not measured for the final
   refit; the CPU/GPU cross-check on CYP2D6 (ρ 0.997) is the only repeatability figure.
8. **The CYP3A4 gap to the leading entries is information, not placement.** The logged ceiling for
   any shift or spread on this ordering is about +0.034 R². Levers that add information (public
   CYP3A4 rows at equal weight, unlabelled blinded-set neighbours) are untested.
9. **Rank is not stable.** Compare ST-RAE across rows.
10. **Public data reaches the file indirectly in two ways.** (a) The output values of CYP2C9 and
   CYP3A4 come from a notebook model trained on challenge plus public rows (item 4). (b) The frozen
   Monroe encoder, used by the `tabpfn_monroe` members and by that notebook, was pretrained by its
   authors on about 81M PM6 molecules and about 1.56M PCBA bioassay molecules. Whether that corpus
   contains CYP assay data or challenge compounds has not been checked; the Monroe log records the
   PCBA/InChIKey overlap check as explicitly out of scope.

## 8. Trials after this submission (not shipped; measured 2026-11-03)

The notebooks were rerun on the cluster with recent code changes. None of the outputs replaces the file above.

- **GNN recipe v2** (12-epoch schedule, lower encoder learning rate, FFN dropout, EMA, winsorised
  descriptors, a public-source flag, window terms), scored out-of-fold over all 4,905 real compounds
  at one fixed epoch, 5 scaffold-disjoint folds, 20-model refit. Pooled OOF: R² 0.187 / 0.446 /
  −0.375 / 0.521; ST-RAE 0.996 / 0.632 / 1.534 / 0.501; ρ 0.531 / 0.652 / 0.414 / 0.760. On the same
  compounds the stack's `tabpfn_monroe` natural member has ρ 0.562 / 0.692 / 0.457 / 0.803 (the folds
  differ, so this is approximate). The final epoch's mean MAE (0.6468) is worse than its pooled
  minimum at epoch 3 (0.6289); CYP1A2 and CYP2D6 degrade from epoch 1 while CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 keep
  improving. The blinded-set columns sit at natural level (means 5.24 / 5.06 / 4.70 / 4.98 against the
  shipped 4.26 / 4.92 / 3.23 / 4.88) and correlate with the shipped file at ρ 0.850 / 0.883 / 0.735 /
  0.890. Verdict: not a submission candidate; CYP2D6 in particular is wide (sd 0.68) for a model with
  ρ 0.41.
- **CYP3A4 did not improve** in any rerun: GNN on the pinned 490 gave R² 0.486 (earlier rows
  0.434–0.485); the Monroe CV moved from 0.7327 to 0.7342 against a split-to-split sd of 0.0135; the
  blinded columns correlate with their predecessors at ρ 0.985 (Monroe) and 0.948 (LightGBM).
- **Protocol changes to be aware of.** The LightGBM notebook now scores on a real-only,
  cluster-disjoint holdout (confirm-half R² 0.228 / 0.289 / 0.051 / 0.415), which is not comparable
  with the random-split figures above. The Monroe notebook still uses random 75/25 splits of pooled
  real and public rows, and still applies its affine recalibration to the saved file, which its own
  nested test rates as worse on three of four isoforms.

## 9. Reproducibility

Code is in `notebooks/`, with SLURM drivers in `slurm/` and artifact checkers in `tools/`. The
members need a GPU for TabPFN; the local workstation has none and TabPFN weights are licence-gated.

1. Held-out arms and the surface: `slurm/cyp_window_labels_array.sh` → `notebooks/run_window_labels_shard.py`;
   check with `tools/check_window_labels_completion.py` (8/8).
2. Blinded-set refit of `natural` and `window`: `slurm/cyp_window_refit.sh` → `notebooks/run_window_refit.py`;
   check with `tools/check_window_refit_completion.py` (4/4, zero label mismatches against the shards).
3. Remaining members: `slurm/cyp_stack_members_array.sh` → `notebooks/run_stack_members_shard.py`;
   check with `tools/check_stack_members_completion.py`.
4. Assembly, which writes a UTC-stamped history copy and a manifest row:

        python notebooks/run_stack_submission.py --assemble --shift CYP3A4=0.172

   A second run of the assembly was bit-identical when checked on the 2026-10-29 build.

The full experiment record, including every board row and the superseded analyses, is in
`outputs/MONROE_EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` (stack and placement), `outputs/GNN_EXPERIMENT_LOG.md` (GNN rows 1–34)
and `outputs/ATOM_JEPA_EXPERIMENT_LOG.md`; strategy is in `PROJECT_PLAN.md`. `python tools/check_docs.py`
gates the logs against the code.
