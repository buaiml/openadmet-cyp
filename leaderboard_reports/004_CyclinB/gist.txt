# Methods summary: direct inhibition track

Team CyclinB. This is a high-level overview of the approach behind our final submission on the
direct inhibition track (pIC50 regression, CYP1A2 / 2C9 / 2D6 / 3A4).

**Proprietary data:** This submission was built partly on internal data, and leveraged internal
foundation models such as MolGPS[^molgps].

## Overview

The pipeline has three stages, run separately for each isoform:

1. **Models.** Several model families, each trained on the public challenge data plus additional public and
   internal data. Each family is the average of a few members that differ in training data or features.
2. **Ensembling.** The family predictions are standardised and blended. The blend sets the order of the test
   compounds.
3. **Metric-aware output calibration.** ST-RAE depends on the level and spread of the predictions as well as their
   order, so the raw blend is not the loss-minimising output. The ordered compounds are mapped onto a fixed set of
   output values chosen to reduce expected ST-RAE.

## Data

- **Challenge data.** Dose–response pIC50 labels with their uncertainty, and the single-concentration screen,
  which covers many compounds that never received a dose–response curve.
- **Public external data.** CYP inhibition data from ChEMBL, patent data, the PubChem CYP screen (AID 1851) and the
  OpenADMET Octant CYP3A4 inhibition dataset[^octant], used as auxiliary training tasks rather than pooled into the
  challenge labels, since those assays differ from the challenge assay.[^overlap]
- **Internal data.**
  - In-house CYP inhibition assays: dose–response curves and single-concentration reads.
  - Internal ADME measurements (logD, microsomal and hepatocyte clearance), used as auxiliary tasks.
- **Representations.** MolGPS[^molgps] embeddings (a 3B-parameter model and three 1B models), Boltz-2[^boltz2] co-folds of
  each compound with its CYP and heme, ECFP fingerprints and RDKit descriptors.

## Model families

- **Gaussian process with a dose–response likelihood.** One GP per isoform over a latent pIC50, with a kernel
  that is a weighted sum of kernels on the MolGPS embeddings, the Boltz-2 co-fold features, fingerprints and
  descriptors. Each data source enters through its own likelihood: dose–response labels directly, with their
  reported uncertainty; single-concentration screen values through a Hill curve at the screened concentration,
  so inactive screen results still inform the model; and the in-house assays on a separate latent correlated with
  the challenge one. CYP2D6 does not use the in-house assay data.
- **Multitask Chemprop.** Directed message-passing networks (Chemprop[^chemprop]) with one output head per assay
  and isoform, so related endpoints shape a shared representation. The members differ in their pretraining on the
  in-house CYP assays, in cleaned versions of the public data sources, and in internal logD and clearance heads.
- **Multitask MLPs on frozen embeddings.** MLPs on fixed MolGPS and related embeddings, trained on the challenge
  endpoints, with pretraining on internal ADME data or public bioassay data depending on the member.
- **CYP1A2 only:** an additional multitask Chemprop family pretrained on the union of the in-house CYP assays. The
  additional tasks helped the other isoforms but not CYP1A2, which therefore gets this separate family.

## Validation

- Five-fold scaffold cross-validation, scored with ST-RAE on out-of-fold predictions. Model choices were also
  checked on a split that holds out close analogue series, which better matches the shift between training and
  test compounds.
- Compounds whose parent structure also appears in the in-house data were excluded from scoring, because no test
  compound has such a match.
- Where family members or stacking weights were selected by cross-validation, the selection was repeated inside
  each outer fold.
- The labelled compounds are a hit-selected subset of the screen, so they contain far fewer inactives than the
  test set is expected to. For CYP2D6, where this difference is largest, we also measured how well models rank the
  screened compounds that have no dose–response label.

## Combining models

Each family's mean prediction is standardised by its out-of-fold standard deviation, and the families are blended
with weights that differ by isoform:

- **CYP1A2:** GP / Chemprop / MLP / union-pretrained Chemprop at 0.25 / 0.30 / 0.25 / 0.20.
- **CYP3A4:** GP / Chemprop / MLP at 0.2 / 0.6 / 0.2.
- **CYP2C9:** a GP + Chemprop stack, blended 0.6 / 0.4 with an equal-weight blend of the three families.
- **CYP2D6:** a GP + Chemprop stack (about 0.7 / 0.3). Screened compounds without a dose–response label are
  included at reduced weight, to correct for the hit selection.

Stack weights are fitted to minimise ST-RAE on out-of-fold predictions.

## Metric-aware output calibration

The output values come from a monotone (order-preserving) map per isoform, fixed before the final ranking is
applied. The ranking is matched onto that set of values by rank: the k-th lowest-ranked compound receives the k-th
lowest value. The map's shape follows from the definition of the metric:

- ST-RAE counts a prediction as correct anywhere inside the measured interval, and inactive compounds have wide
  intervals. Predictions far below about pIC50 3 therefore cannot help an inactive compound, but they cost a great
  deal on any active compound placed there, so low predictions are raised to about 3.
- For an isoform where most compounds are inactive (CYP2D6), the output that minimises expected ST-RAE holds most compounds at a common
  low value and spreads out the predicted actives above it.

Some isoforms received small further adjustments to the calibrated values:

- **CYP2C9 and CYP2D6:** predictions were moved toward their analogue series' mean deviation from the overall mean,
  using series reconstructed by clustering. On CYP2D6 this is applied only to predicted actives, and only upward.
- **CYP1A2:** the most potent 20% of predictions were compressed toward their mean, and the lowest values spread
  slightly downward.
